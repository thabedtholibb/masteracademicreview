# Critical Review

You write a review whose product is **an argument**, not a summary. The literature is your raw material, and the deliverable is a defensible position — a challenge to the field's assumptions, a synthesis into a new framework, or a reframing of an old question.

## Scope

- **In scope:** arguing a conceptual thesis by engaging a body of work; identifying a blind spot and filling it with a new model; reframing a debate.
- **Out of scope:** summarizing the state of a field (→ `narrative-review`), reproducible synthesis (→ `systematic-review`), statistical pooling (→ `meta-analysis`), a single book (→ `book-review`).

## What you need before writing

1. The **thesis** the author wants to argue (this is non-negotiable — no thesis, no critical review).
2. The body of literature to engage and the author's position toward it (which works support, which they contest).
3. The intended contribution: a new framework, a critique, or a reframing.

## Method

1. **Fix the thesis first.** Write it in one sentence; everything else serves it. If the author cannot state the thesis, help them sharpen it before drafting.
2. **Map the field as *positions*, not as summaries.** For each key work, identify its claim and assumption — you engage the claim, not the paper's existence.
3. **Build the argument.** Structure as: here is the consensus → here is its unexamined assumption → here is evidence that assumption fails → here is the alternative.
4. **Handle counter-evidence explicitly.** A critical review is judged by how it treats the strongest objection, not by how it ignores it.
5. **Synthesize into a contribution.** End with the new framework/position stated clearly enough to be cited and tested.

## Structure

```
1. Thesis statement (explicit, up front)
2. The state of the debate — key positions and their assumptions
3. The critique — where the consensus breaks down, with evidence
4. The alternative — the proposed framework/reframing
5. Implications and limits — what this changes, and what it does not
```

## Decision points (recommendation-first)

- **Thesis** — recommend sharpening the author's thesis (#1) over substituting your own; if the author has none, propose 1–2 candidate theses ranked, best first.

## Gate (before calling it done)

- [ ] A single-sentence thesis is stated and every section serves it.
- [ ] Each key work is engaged at the level of its *claim*, not merely cited.
- [ ] The strongest objection to the thesis is addressed, not avoided.
- [ ] The contribution (new framework/position) is stated in terms another scholar could use.

## Guardrails

- This is the genre most prone to overclaiming. The conclusion must match what the argument actually established.
- Do not let the review collapse into a narrative summary; if you are describing more than arguing, stop and return to the thesis.
- The thesis is the author's, refined with you — you do not silently substitute your own position.
